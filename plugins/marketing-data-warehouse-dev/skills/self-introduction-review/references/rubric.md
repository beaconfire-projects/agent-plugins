# Rubric — 10-part self-introduction

Source: company "Self-Intro 10-Part Structure" (parts, order, content, ~5–6 min spoken) plus a required AI-usage element requested by the Trainer/AM team. Weights, score levels, bands and hard checks below are a **proposed scoring convention** layered on that structure; the company document does not define numeric weights. Revise this file when the company sets official ones.

## Parts and weights (total 100)

| # | Part | Must cover | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening | Kind of systems the candidate builds, industry focus, most recent core project | 8 |
| 2 | Business context | What business problem the system solves | 8 |
| 3 | Why the project existed | Pain points and constraints | 8 |
| 4 | Overall approach | How the team delivered and controlled risk | 8 |
| 5 | Project outcome (team result) | Headline metric, credited to the team | 8 |
| 6 | Your role | When joined, what worked on, one representative feature | 12 |
| 7 | Ownership and boundaries | How scope grew and where it stopped | 8 |
| 8 | Your result | A concrete result the candidate contributed to | 8 |
| 9 | Challenges (2–3, hooks only) | One line per challenge with an offer to go deeper | 8 |
| 10 | Summary and direction | Skill keywords and where the candidate wants to go next | 6 |
| AI | **Required: AI-assisted work** | Statement of how the candidate uses AI in their own work (see below) | 10 |
| — | Overall quality | See below | 8 |

## Per-part score levels

Multiply the weight by the level:

- **Full (100%)**: the required content is clearly present, specific, and in the right place.
- **Mostly (75%)**: present but thin or slightly generic, or one required element missing.
- **Partial (50%)**: touched on vaguely, in the wrong place, or mixed into another part.
- **Minimal (25%)**: a passing mention that gives the listener nothing usable.
- **Missing (0%)**: not said.

Status mapping: Full/Mostly = Covered; Partial/Minimal = Partial; Missing = Missing.

Part-specific checks:
- 1: no bare technology-stack opening; names the system type, industry and recent project.
- 2/3: business problem and pain points come before implementation; constraints are named, not just "legacy".
- 4: states a delivery method and risk control (phasing, parallel run, rollback, testing), not a tool list.
- 5: a headline outcome (metric, date, or concrete result) attributed to the team/program. A claim without any attribution gets at most Mostly.
- 6: gives join time, scope and one representative feature explained at business level. Merely listing technologies is Partial at best.
- 7: shows how ownership grew and a boundary (reviews, approvals, what the candidate did not own). Unbounded solo claims get at most Mostly.
- 8: a concrete personal result tied to the representative feature; team result restated as personal gets Partial and triggers the attribution flag.
- 9: 2–3 challenges, each one line, ending with an offer to go deeper. One challenge = Mostly at best; none = Missing; full solution walkthroughs = Partial (hooks should not be exhausted).
- 10: skill keywords supported by the story plus a direction; generic "hard-working" language does not count.
- AI (required element, 10): the introduction must say how the candidate uses AI in their own work, for example AI-based SDLC, copilot-assisted development, using AI to bootstrap development and testing, or using ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Cursor to help complete specific work. It can appear anywhere (usually parts 6–8 or 10), and counts only if the candidate says it, not if the project merely is an AI product.
  - Full: names the AI tool or practice, the SDLC activity it helped with (such as coding, tests, debugging, design, docs, review) and what it helped achieve or how the candidate used it.
  - Mostly: names a tool or practice and a task, but no effect or no personal role.
  - Partial: a generic claim ("I use AI tools", "familiar with AI") with no task, or AI mentioned only as part of an unrelated technology list.
  - Minimal: a passing keyword that gives the listener nothing usable.
  - Missing: no statement of using AI in their own work. Building an AI product or agent (for example "an AI agent tool") does not count by itself; note it as a project domain, not as AI-assisted work.
  - If missing or weak: deduct as above, and the advice must tell the candidate that this element is required and give a spoken line that adds it. Build lines only from stated facts and mark unknown tool or task `[confirm]`. Never invent tools, tasks or results. Example directions for the Trainer/AM: talk about an AI-based SDLC, copilot-assisted development, using AI to bootstrap development and testing, or using ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini for a named task.

## Overall quality (10)

Score 0–8 across: order follows parts 1→10 without going backwards (3); project and personal content kept separate and team vs personal credit correct (2); business-first with technology only where it explains value (1); length and pacing near 5–6 minutes, natural and spoken rather than read (2). If length or delivery cannot be assessed, award those sub-points proportionally from the assessable content and say so.

## Bands

| Total | Band | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 85–100 | Meets standard | Ready to use; polish only |
| 70–84 | Needs improvement | Structure mostly present; fix the listed gaps and re-record |
| 50–69 | Below standard | Several parts missing or weak; rework before client use |
| <50 | Not structured | Does not follow the 10-part structure |

## Hard checks (flag; cap band at "Needs improvement" if any triggers)

- Part 6 or part 8 missing entirely.
- AI-assisted work missing entirely (the required element scores 0).
- A team/program result presented as the candidate's own.
- Technology list used in place of project/business explanation in the first half.
- Facts that appear invented or contradict themselves (flag as "needs verification"; do not accuse).
- Duration under 3 minutes or over 8 minutes when known.

## Scoring discipline

- Score only what was said. Evidence required for every score above 0.
- Round part scores to the nearest whole number; the total is their sum.
- Do not add or remove parts for the candidate's background; all 10 apply to every self-introduction.
